A

Geometry, walls and the shape of the domain

A0Exp 0 — is anything moving on its own?no gravity
Spawn one body at (50, 50) at rest. Step five times.Position, velocity and q stay bit-identical. Every force law must vanish at v = 0 or be proportional to v — and nothing pulls.5 steps → (50,50), (0,0), q=0
A1A2/A3 — sweep the box: where are the walls?walls at W/2, H/2
Spawn bodies across a wide range of x at rest; one step.Everything with x > 50 is snapped to exactly 50. Sweeping the world size moves the limit with the half-extent: W=37 clamps at 18.5, W=200 at 100.W=100 → clamp 50
A2A4/D5 — does the wall care about the radius?centre only
Same approach, four radii: 0.01, 1, 5, 20.A body with radius 20 still clamps at x = 50, not at 30. The wall is a bound on the centre; bodies overlap each other freely at the wall.r=20 → clamp at 50
A3A6 — is there a wall on the other side?x<0 unbounded
Release a body at x = 0.1 with a small negative velocity and step.It crosses x = 0 without slowing and keeps going to x = −0.0199. There is no left wall, no floor, no ceiling. The domain is a half-space.reaches x = −0.0199
A4A5/A7 — how elastic is the wall?e = 1 exactly
Fire a body at the wall at v = 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 40 and read the outgoing speed.The outgoing velocity is the exact negation of the post-drag velocity: 10 → −(10 − 0.5 − 0.003) = −9.497. Perfectly elastic, no loss.v=10 → v=−9.497
A5A7 — when during the step is the wall applied?per substep
1 × step(dt=0.1) versus 10 × step(dt=0.01) for a body that bounces.Bit-identical, and the bounce is seen mid-call: so the wall is resolved on every substep, after the position integration.identical to the last bit
B

Free motion — two independent decelerations

B1B1 — how much does a body slow down per substep?0.5v²/m + 0.003
Single body, m = 1, one substep of h = 0.01, speeds 0.5 → 100.After subtracting the mass-dependent term, a constant 0.003 is left at every speed. So there are two brakes: quadratic drag plus a fixed 0.3 / s².h=0.01 → 0.003 left over
B2F — which brake depends on mass?floor term does not
Hold v = 10, sweep the mass from 0.25 to 256.The quadratic part scales as 1/m; the extra 0.003 is identical at every mass — a Coulomb term, not a viscous one.Δv = 0.5v²h/m + 0.003
B3F2 — is that second brake really floor friction?acts everywhere
Same body, same speed, at y = 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50.The deceleration is 0.8 = 0.5·1²/1 + 0.3 at every height, including far above the floor. It is a constant deceleration of 0.3 in the −v̂ direction.Δv/h = 0.8 at all y
B4B — along which axis does the floor term bite?along −v̂
Give a body v = (8, −4) and take one substep.The loss is collinear with v: Δv = −h(0.5|v|/m + 0.3)v/|v|. Not a per-component friction.Δv = −0.1068, −0.0534
B5F2 — what happens to a body slower than 0.3h?sign flips every substep
Start at v = 0.001, 0.01, 10−6 and step ten times.The 0.3 brake is applied unconditionally, so a body slower than 0.3·h = 0.003 overshoots and reverses on every substep. 0.001 ends at +0.001000074996.v=0.001 → +0.001000075
B6B — does position use the old or the new velocity?symplectic: x += vₕ·h
From (5,5) with v = (−20, 0), one substep.x lands on 5 − 0.17997 = 4.82003 = 5 + (−17.997)·0.01: the updated velocity moves the body.x = 4.82003
C

Pairwise force law

C1C — what is the distance exponent?1/d³, not 1/d²
Two bodies at rest, m = 1, d = 2.5 → 80, one substep.|a|·d³ = 1.000000 at every distance. On a log–log plot the points sit exactly on the 1/d³ line and nowhere near 1/d².a = 1/d³
C2C — how do the masses enter?F = G·mᵢmᵣ/d³
Sweep mᵢ, mᵣ over {1,2,4,10} at d = 10 and read both accelerations.aᵢmᵢ = −aᵣmᵣ always: a true equal-and-opposite force. And a body feels G·m(other)/d³ — its own mass does not enter.mᵢmᵣ=100 → a = 100×
C3C2 — the colour law: all nine pairingssame = attract, differ = repel
All 3×3 colour combinations at d = 10, m = 1.Identical magnitude, opposite sign: equal colours attract, different colours repel, symmetrically in both orderings.a = ±1/d³
C4C — does the radius change the force?centre distance only
Four radius pairs (0.1, 2, 3) at the same centre distance d = 10.All give a = 0.001. The law uses the centre-to-centre distance, never a surface gap; and there is no cut-off (still measurable at d = 199).a = 0.001 for every radius
D

Collisions

D1D3 — what happens to the velocities?they swap; mass ignored
Head-on, r = 20 (so the mutual force is negligible), v = ±0.2, gap 0.05.The two bodies exchange velocity vectors — a 100:1 mass pair behaves exactly like a 1:1 pair. Neither momentum nor energy is conserved.m = 100:1 → still swaps
D2D4 — the whole vector, or only the normal component?whole vector
Oblique approach: vᵢ = (0.2, 0.05), vᵣ = (−0.1, 0.02).Both components swap: (0.2,0.05) ↔ (−0.1,0.02). The entire vector is exchanged, not just the part along the line of centres. A 45° collision does the same.full vector exchanged
D3D4 — how are overlapping bodies pushed apart?split half/half, no mass
Overlap bodies by 4 units and step, at m = 1:1, 4:1 and 100:1; also r = 20 vs 10.Both move by exactly half the overlap regardless of mass and radii: (100,136) → (98,138) in every case.each moves overlap/2
D4D4 — does it fire when they are separating?unconditional
Overlap bodies moving apart: v = (0.2, 0.5).They swap anyway → (0.49575, 0.1968). There is no approaching-check; two overlapping bodies at rest stay at rest.swaps while separating
D5D5/E — the hidden twist: contact kills the forceno force while touching
Two bodies at rest, r = 5 each (touching at d = 10). Compare d = 10 and d = 10.001.At d ≤ 10 the mutual force is exactly zero; at 10.001 it jumps to 1/d³. Contact replaces the force with the swap. Found only when a 6-body run diverged by precisely this term.a = 0 for d ≤ rᵢ+rᵣ
E

Charge

E1E — what does q do to a body?a = −0.4·q·r
Set q via PATCH, v = 0, one substep, at (10,10), (20,5), (−10,10), (40,40) and masses 0.5–16.The acceleration is −0.4·q·r — a linear spring to the origin, identical at every mass. q enters no other term.k = 0.4, mass-free
E2E/F — how does q build up?5.0 per second above v*
Sweep the starting speed across 1.48 → 2.05 and read q after one substep.The test is on the speed after the drag step: 1.4956 → nothing, 1.5054 → gain. The rate is 5.0 per second (0.05 per 0.01 s), and it is a rate, not a step constant.|v| > 1.5 → dq/dt = 5
E3E2 — does q ever stop growing?saturates at 10
Hold a body above the threshold for 600 substeps; then PATCH q = 99.q climbs 0.05, 0.10, … and sticks at exactly 10.0. PATCH q = 99 is accepted by the API but clamped to 10 on the next step. Without this cap a single-body run diverged by 0.33.q ← min(q + 5h, 10)
F

Integration

F1F3 — how big is one integration step?h = 0.01 s
Run dt = 0.1, 0.05, 0.02, 0.01 as single calls from the same state.All four give the identical result (21.2909082076, 9.41589576). dt = 0.005 and below give something else — so the substep is capped at 0.01 s.dt = 0.1 ≡ 10 × 0.01
F2F3 — are the substeps equal?ceil(dt/0.01) equal parts
Compare 1 × step(0.015) against 2 × step(0.0075).Bit-identical, and 15 × step(0.001) differs. So dt is split into n = ceil(dt/0.01) equal substeps, not n full ones plus a remainder.0.015 = 2 × 0.0075
F3F3 — the exact order inside one substeppinned bit-exactly
A body at v = (8,−4) with charge feedback, traced for ten substeps.The pipeline below reproduces 7.818431 / −3.909216 to the last digit. Each step of the pipeline can be re-proved by a targeted experiment.reproduces to 2e−16
G

API behaviour and edge cases

G1G — what does alive:false do?freezes the body
Set alive = false on a moving body and step.Position, velocity and q all stop dead; the body exerts and receives nothing. It is simply removed from the simulation.frozen, not colliding
G2G — impulse and forceJ/m, integrated properly
apply impulse (3,−6) to m = 4; then apply force (4,0) for 0.05 s to m = 2.Impulse gives exactly J/m. The force runs through the whole substep loop — the 0.3 brake and the drag are still applied, giving 0.087975691 instead of a naive 0.1.F(4,0)·0.05s → v = 0.0879757
G3D — a three-body collision chainindex-order exchange
Three bodies in a row, radii 1, 1.2, 0.8, gaps 0.1 and 0.2, moving inward.All three are in contact at once and the velocities travel down the chain in index order, exactly reproduced by a single i<j pass.(0.5,−0.3,−0.7) → swapped
G4G — two bodies at the identical pointdegenerate but stable
Spawn two bodies at exactly (100,100) with opposite velocities.No crash, no NaN: the velocities still swap and the bodies are pushed apart to rᵢ+rᵣ using some fallback direction — the only rule left un-nailed.d = 0 handled

Validation — how much do we trust it?

H1H — does the rebuilt world match the server?bit-exact for 2000 steps
Re-run the model against the server for thousands of substeps and log the error.One body: 7.3e−16 for 2000 substeps — machine precision. Several bodies stay there too until a contact test finally flips.max err 7.3e−16
H2H — then why does it drift later?chaos, not a missing rule
Control: perturb our own model by one ULP and re-run — 12 random scenarios.One ULP of divergence grows to the same magnitude as the model-vs-server difference (median 3.0e−3 vs 2.0e−3). The Coulomb overshoot plus a discrete contact predicate makes the world chaotic.1 ULP → same blow-up
